## Section 2 - New Issues (Adversarial Hunt)

### R2-1 - P1 - Multi-product transfer completion can still AB-BA deadlock on advisory locks

File: `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:175`

`complete()` processes `$transfer->lines` in stored order at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:175-199`, and each line calls `StockAdjustmentService::receive()` at `:182-189`. `receive()` acquires exactly one product advisory lock for that line at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Domain/Services/StockAdjustmentService.php:56-57`, and because it is `pg_advisory_xact_lock` the lock is held until the outer transaction commits; `ProductCostLock` documents transaction-scoped advisory locks at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Domain/Services/ProductCostLock.php:13-17` and implements `pg_advisory_xact_lock(...)` at `:46-48`.

First-principles deadlock: transfer T1 with line order `[A, B]` can acquire advisory lock A in its first `receive()`, while transfer T2 with line order `[B, A]` acquires advisory lock B. T1 then waits for B and T2 waits for A. The sorted-order defense in `ProductCostLock` only applies within one `acquire()` call because it sorts the received `$productIds` at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Domain/Services/ProductCostLock.php:42-47`; `complete()` never passes all transfer product IDs in one call, so that defense is bypassed. The same pattern exists in `cancel()` for in-transit transfers because it loops `$transfer->lines` and calls `receive()` per line at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:257-276`.

`attempts: 3` on the outer `complete()` transaction at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:167-236` is useful recovery, but it is not a lock-ordering proof. Both transactions retry the same stored line order, so under repeated overlap the same AB-BA cycle remains possible.

Concrete fix: inject `ProductCostLock` into `StockTransferService` and wrap the whole completion critical section, before the per-line `receive()` loop and before `capitalizeTransferCost()`, in one `costLock->acquire($transfer->tenant_id, $transfer->company_id, $transfer->lines->pluck('product_id')->all(), ...)` call. `ProductCostLock` will sort the complete product set at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Domain/Services/ProductCostLock.php:42-47`; nested single-product `receive()` and `recordCostAdjustment()` calls can re-enter already-held transaction locks. Apply the same pattern to `cancel()` when it receives multiple line products.

### R2-2 - P1 - Product-row/stock-level inversion still exists between cost adjustment and pure sale paths

File: `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/WeightedAverageCostService.php:561`

The lock-ordering claim is only partly true. For the newly changed `StockAdjustmentService::receive()`, `adjust()`, and destination side of `transfer()`, I did not find a product-row lock after `lockStockLevel()`; those methods take the advisory lock and then lock stock-level rows at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Domain/Services/StockAdjustmentService.php:56-57`, `:226-228`, `:261-262`, and `:448-449`. Among advisory-lock participants, same-product concurrency is serialized by `ProductCostLock`.

But the broader product-row/stock-level inversion is not safe just because a per-product advisory lock exists: `recordSale()` is explicitly outside the seam and takes `stock_levels` then `products` (`lockForUpdate()` on `StockLevel` at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/WeightedAverageCostService.php:269-277`, then `lockForUpdate()` on `Product` at `:279-285`). The architecture test pins that pure decrement methods must not take `ProductCostLock` at `apps/api/tests/Architecture/InventoryCostLockCoverageTest.php:51-56` and asserts they must not contain `costLock->acquire` at `:75-88`. `recordCostAdjustment()` does the opposite order: it acquires the advisory lock at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/WeightedAverageCostService.php:558-559`, then locks the product row at `:560-565`, then locks all stock-level rows at `:567-576`.

First-principles deadlock: a sale can hold the stock-level row and wait for the product row, while a cost adjustment holds the product row and waits for the same stock-level row. The sale has no advisory lock by design, so `ProductCostLock` cannot serialize that pair.

Concrete fix: make `recordCostAdjustment()` use the same row order as `recordPurchase()` and `recordReturn()`: advisory lock first, then stock-level row locks, then product row. `recordPurchase()` demonstrates that order at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/WeightedAverageCostService.php:99-131`; `recordReturn()` demonstrates the same order at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/WeightedAverageCostService.php:376-404`. If `recordCostAdjustment()` needs the product row for current cost, lock it after the stock-level rows and compute from the locked product.

### R2-3 - P2 - Persisted transfer-cost allocations can still lose residual after truncation to 4 decimals

File: `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:455`

The residual is assigned at the 6-decimal working scale at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:447-453`, but the persisted line value is truncated to 4 decimals with `CurrencyScale::bcformat($allocated, self::QTY_SCALE)` at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:455`. `CurrencyScale::bcformat()` uses `bcadd($str, '0', $scale)` at `apps/api/app/Shared/Domain/CurrencyScale.php:85-109`, and its own comment says `bcformat()` truncates toward zero at `apps/api/app/Shared/Domain/CurrencyScale.php:147-153`. The database and model store both `transfer_cost` and `allocated_transfer_cost` at 4 decimals: `StockTransferLine` casts `allocated_transfer_cost` as `decimal:4` at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Domain/StockTransferLine.php:44-55`, and the migration defines `transfer_cost` and `allocated_transfer_cost` as `decimal(15, 4)` at `apps/api/database/migrations/2026_05_28_120000_create_stock_transfers_table.php:44-78`.

Concrete counterexample from the current algorithm: equal allocation of `100.0000` over seven cost-bearing lines computes six non-final shares as `14.285714` and the residual as `14.285716` at lines `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:426-453`. Persisting all seven through `CurrencyScale::bcformat(..., 4)` at `:455` stores `14.2857` seven times, summing to `99.9999`, not `100.0000`. The new regression test covers three lines at `apps/api/tests/Feature/Inventory/InventoryTransferServiceTest.php:469-517`, but it does not catch this seven-line truncation case.

Concrete fix: reconcile at the same scale that is persisted, or persist allocation at the same scale used for WAC capitalization. If the schema remains `decimal(15,4)`, compute and store all but the final cost-bearing line at 4 decimals, then assign the final persisted line `transferCost - sum(persistedOthers)` at 4 decimals and pass that same persisted amount to `recordCostAdjustment()`.

### R2-4 - P1 - Transfer initiation still locks product rows before stock-level rows

File: `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:320`

The completion path no longer takes a product row lock before the advisory lock, but `moveSourceToInTransit()` still locks each `Product` row at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:319-324`, then locks the source `StockLevel` row at `:329-334`, then calls the pure decrement `issue()` path at `:350-357`. `issue()` itself locks the stock level at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Domain/Services/StockAdjustmentService.php:134-135` and, by architecture contract, does not take the advisory lock (`apps/api/tests/Architecture/InventoryCostLockCoverageTest.php:51-56` and `:75-88`).

This remains a product-row-before-stock-level order on a pure decrement path. It can deadlock with `recordPurchase()`, which takes the advisory lock and then locks stock level before product at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/WeightedAverageCostService.php:99-131`, and with `recordSale()`, which locks stock level before product at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/WeightedAverageCostService.php:269-285`. `attempts: 3` on `initiate()` at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:94-156` retries the victim but does not remove the inverted order.

Concrete fix: do not `lockForUpdate()` the product row in `moveSourceToInTransit()` just to snapshot `cost_price`; load it without a row lock, or move the snapshot read after the source stock-level lock in a consistent stock-level-then-product order. If a locked cost snapshot is required, make initiation participate in the same sorted `ProductCostLock` seam before any product row lock, but that changes the current pure-decrement contract and should be documented in `InventoryCostLockCoverageTest`.

### Question C - Allocation edge cases

The "last line is a skipped zero-share line" case is handled by the current structure: zero or negative allocations are skipped at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:432-436`, and residual is assigned to `count($allocations) - 1`, i.e. the last cost-bearing allocation, at `:439-451`. Therefore a zero-share final transfer line does not prevent the previous cost-bearing line from absorbing the residual.

The "all lines have zero share" case is mostly handled for normal transfer inputs because when `$totalWeight` is zero the code falls back to equal-per-line allocation at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:426-430`, and transfer initiation rejects an empty line list and non-positive quantities at `apps/api/app/Modules/Inventory/Application/Services/StockTransferService.php:78-80` and `:139-142`. The remaining correctness hole is R2-3: if every computed equal share truncates to zero at the working/persisted scale, `$allocations` can become empty at `:418-436`, `$lastIndex` becomes `-1` at `:439`, and no residual is ever assigned because the second loop at `:443-472` has nothing to process.

## Section 3 - Summary

The fixes move the foundation in the right direction: the completion pre-lock was removed, the read-modify-write sites now read under `FOR UPDATE`, lifecycle transactions retry, and the old upsert seam now goes through `ProductCostLock`. The remaining risk is still concurrency-critical rather than cosmetic. Multi-product transfer completion and cancellation acquire transaction-scoped advisory locks one product at a time in arbitrary persisted order, and row-lock ordering remains inconsistent between cost adjustment and lock-free sale/initiation paths. The allocation fix also reconciles at a higher working scale but persists at 4 decimals, leaving residual loss cases outside the new three-line regression.
