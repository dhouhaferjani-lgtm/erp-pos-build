## Nest-guard design note (item 3)

The orchestrator asks whether disabling `RefreshDatabase`'s wrapping transaction in `StockTransferEdgeCasesTest` / `InventoryTransferServiceTest` was the only way, or whether the guard could compare against a captured base level as `InventoryGlPostingBuffer::flushIfOutermost` reasons about level 1.

What the code actually constrains:

1. **The absolute level-1 rule is the *buffer's*, not the receipt service's.** `InventoryGlPostingBuffer.php:56-68` posts only at `DB::transactionLevel() === 1`; at `> 1` it returns `[]` and registers a leak alarm, at `0` it throws. That buffer is shared by at least eight writers (POS projection `PosCoreReceiptProjection.php:204`, `DeliveryNoteService.php:61`, `ReturnNoteService.php:80`, `RefundService.php:48`, `ReceiptReturnService.php:104`, `ReturnScrapWriteOffService.php:72`, `InvoiceController.php:91`, `DeliveryNoteController.php:72`). So a *relative* baseline in the receipt service alone would buy nothing — the GL still would not post at level 2 — and making the **buffer** baseline-relative would let a genuinely nested caller post GL inside a foreign transaction, which is precisely the leak the buffer exists to prevent. I would oppose that change; it is not cheaper, it is a cross-lane weakening.
2. **The cost is not novel, and not caused by `connectionsToTransact(): []` per se.** 26 test classes in the repo already do exactly this for the same reason, and one of them documents it verbatim: `CountCorrectionGlPostingTest.php:50-53` — "`InventoryGlPostingBuffer::flushIfOutermost()` posts only at `DB::transactionLevel() === 1`. RefreshDatabase's wrapping transaction would …". The two adapted classes joined an existing convention. The expensive part is the companion `RefreshDatabaseState::$migrated = false` in `setUp()` (`StockTransferEdgeCasesTest.php:65`, `InventoryTransferServiceTest.php:81`), which forces a `migrate:fresh` **per test**. I measured it: the whole class is **1m22.9s / 27 tests** on SQLite and **4.3s for a single method** — i.e. ~3.0s marginal per test, essentially all schema rebuild (tenancy measured 2m18 on PG).
3. **There is one genuinely cheaper *guard* variant, and it has a real trade-off.** Narrow the refusal to the case where value is at stake: leave `transact()` nest-tolerant and instead require `DB::transactionLevel() === 1` at the point that already detects scrap — `StockTransferReceiptService.php:133-145`, beside the mode and account preflights. At that point still nothing has been written (locks only), so it remains a pre-write typed refusal; and both classes' `complete()` calls are damage-free, so they could keep `RefreshDatabase`'s transaction and the ~2 minutes disappear. The cost is that "may I nest?" becomes payload-dependent — a future internal caller would find `complete()` nestable and `receive(damaged)` not — and the invariant a reader must hold in their head splits in two.

**Recommendation.** Keep the guard exactly as written at `:97-99`. One absolute sentence ("this service owns the root transaction") is the cheapest thing to *keep true*, it matches the buffer's own absolute rule, and it makes the S1 change of risk profile (pre-S1 nesting `complete()` was harmless because transfers posted no GL) impossible to reintroduce. Buy the runtime back on the test side instead, where it is free of correctness risk: either split the two classes so only the receipt/complete-bearing cases live in a non-transactional class, or replace the per-test `migrate:fresh` with truncation-based isolation (no DDL per test). Either reduces 27 × migrate to 1 × migrate without touching the guard.
